Klastro lets you announce that an assessment is webcam-proctored. The setting is made
**per invitation**, when you send it. This page explains how to turn it on, what the candidate is told,
and what remains your responsibility.

## Require a webcam on an invitation

<Steps>

1. Open **Assess**, then **Invitations**, and click **Invite candidate**.

2. Fill in the candidate, template and expiry date
   (see [Send and manage invitations](/en/recruiters/send-invitations/)).

3. Under **Session settings**, turn on **Require webcam**
   ("Enable live proctoring via webcam").

4. Click **Send invitation**.

</Steps>

The setting is off by default. It then appears on the invitation page, in the **Settings** card, on the
**Webcam required** line (**Yes** or **No**).

:::caution
The setting cannot be changed after sending. To change your mind, revoke the invitation and send a new
one, as long as the candidate has not accepted it.
:::

## What the candidate sees

On their invitation page, before starting, the candidate sees:

- the **Webcam** line, set to **Required** or **Optional**;
- when a webcam is required, an extra line under **Important Information**:
  "Webcam access will be required".

The candidate therefore learns about the condition **before** clicking **Start Assessment**, never
mid-assessment. The candidate's side is described in
[The lab in assessment mode](/en/lab/assessment-mode/).

## What you see during the assessment

Live supervision from the dashboard goes through the candidate's **read-only terminal** and the progress
of their scenarios (see [Supervise a run live](/en/recruiters/live-supervision/)).

The dashboard has no video player for the webcam, and the candidate report contains no image or video.
Don't present the webcam to the candidate as a recording that goes into their file.

## Privacy: what falls to you

When you turn on webcam proctoring, your organization is the **data controller** for that data; Klastro
acts on your behalf as a processor. In practice, before requiring a webcam, it is up to you to:

- hold a valid **legal basis**. In a hiring context, the candidate's free and informed consent is in
  practice the only one you can reliably rely on;
- **inform the candidate**, before the assessment, of the assessment methods and techniques used (under
  French law, articles L1221-8 and L1221-9 of the Labour Code); no method they were not told about may
  be used;
- assess whether a **data protection impact assessment** (GDPR article 35) is required, since systematic
  monitoring of candidates is among the criteria to consider.

:::tip
Only require a webcam if it genuinely serves your process. Reading the terminal is usually enough to
judge a candidate's work, and every piece of data you don't collect is one obligation fewer.
:::

Klastro's real-time streams are hosted by Klastro on its own infrastructure in the European Union: no
stream is handed to a third-party provider.

A candidate who wants to exercise their data rights (access, erasure…) must contact your organization,
which ran the assessment. A request Klastro receives directly is passed on to you.
