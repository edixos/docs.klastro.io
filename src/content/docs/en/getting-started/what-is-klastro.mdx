Klastro is a platform of **hands-on cloud-native labs** that open right in your browser. Every lab gives you a real environment — a Kubernetes cluster, one or more virtual machines — provisioned fresh for you and destroyed when it ends. There is nothing to install: a terminal, and sometimes a VS Code editor or web apps, is all you need.

The same platform serves three purposes:

- **Self-paced learning**: guided tutorials, graded challenges and open environments, at your own pace.
- **Technical skills assessment**: a recruiter invites a candidate to a timed, proctored assessment, then reads their report.
- **Instructor-led training**: an instructor moves a class through scenario after scenario and follows every participant.

## Scenarios, sessions and tasks

Everything starts with a **scenario**. A scenario describes an environment (the kind of cluster or machines), what is preinstalled on it and, most of the time, a list of **tasks** to complete. Some scenarios are official, written and maintained by the Klastro team (**Official** badge); others are authored by your organization in Scenario Studio (**Studio** badge).

When you launch a scenario, Klastro creates a **session**: your own copy of the environment, isolated from everyone else’s. The session page shows the setup as it happens (**Reserving your environment…**, **Starting the machines…**, **Setting up your lab…**), then turns into your workspace: instructions on one side, terminal on the other.

A session lives until you end it, or until the lifetime set by the scenario runs out. You can pause it and pick up exactly where you left off later. See [Manage your sessions](/en/learners/manage-sessions/) for the details.

Depending on the scenario, your work is validated **against the environment itself**: Klastro inspects the cluster, not an answer you typed in. If the resource you were asked for exists and behaves as expected, the task passes.

## Product types

In the app, every scenario belongs to one of these four types. They share the same real environments, but not the same rules.

| Type | What it is | Hints | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tutorials** | Guided, step by step. You follow the instructions to the end, and you can check each step. | Free | None |
| **Challenges** | A real task, graded. Solve it yourself; your work is graded when you submit it. | Cost points | Yes, kept in your history |
| **Playgrounds** | A blank environment, no instructions and nothing to submit. Explore the tool freely. | None | None |
| **Assessments** | Exam material, run only inside a timed, proctored assessment you are invited to. | Cost points, and every reveal shows on the report | Yes, sent to the recruiter |

Tutorials, challenges and playgrounds are launched from the **Learn** menu. An assessment-type scenario is never launched on its own: a recruiter adds it to an assessment, then invites you to it.

**Lab** is the general word for any environment you launch, whatever its type.

## Organizations, roles and plans

In Klastro you always work **inside an organization**: your company, your school, your community or a personal workspace. The organization holds a plan, credits and members. You can belong to several organizations and switch between them.

Labs draw **credits**, metered on the time they actually run and weighted by environment type. Paused time is not counted, and a provisioning failure is not billed.

At a high level, the plans break down like this:

- **Community** — free: tutorials, challenges and playgrounds on the free scenarios in the catalog.
- **Pro** — every **Pro** scenario, for each member holding a Pro seat.
- **Instructor** — everything in Pro, plus Scenario Studio and instructor-led training (cohorts and tracks).
- **Enterprise** — by contract: proctored hiring assessments, every scenario for every member, Scenario Studio.

Plans, seats and credits are explained in detail in [Plans and credits](/en/billing/plans-and-credits/).
