Every Klastro scenario is a list of tasks. Klastro does not grade your commands
or your typing: it **inspects the live state of your environment** — whether the
Deployment exists, whether the Pod is running, whether the file has the right
content — and compares it with what the task asks for. This page explains how
that works and how it differs between tutorials, challenges and assessments.

## How a check works

When a task is checked, Klastro looks at your environment at that moment and runs
the task's checks against it. A task usually has several checks; it **passes**
when all of its required checks pass.

- **Passed** means your environment, right now, is in the state the task
  describes. How you got there does not matter.
- **Failed** means at least one check did not match. It is not an error: fix the
  environment and the task can pass on the next check.

Checking takes a few seconds, sometimes longer on a busy or large environment.
If Klastro cannot reach part of your environment while checking, it keeps trying
by itself rather than failing the task.

:::tip
Because Klastro grades the end state, a correct result reached by a different
route than the instructions suggest still passes. Undoing your own work before a
check, on the other hand, makes it fail.
:::

## Tutorials, challenges and assessments

The same scenario format is used everywhere, but each kind of lab checks your
work at a different moment.

| | Tutorial | Challenge | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task list called | **Steps** | **Tasks** | **Tasks** |
| When work is checked | Whenever you click **Check this step** | When you click **Finish & submit** on the last task | Automatically, when the assessment ends |
| Feedback while working | Yes, per step | No | No |
| Hints | **Guided steps**, free | **Hints**, cost points | **Hints**, cost points (cost hidden) |
| Points shown to you | No | Yes | No |

A playground has no tasks, checks or hints.

## Move between tasks

Click any task in the list to open it, or use **Previous** and **Next** at the
bottom of the pane. You can work on tasks in any order. The header shows how many
are done, for example **3 of 8**, and a thin bar under it fills as you progress.

To give the instructions more width, click the list toggle in the header
(**Hide tasks** or **Hide steps**). The list shrinks to its numbers and stays
usable.

## Check a tutorial step

<Steps>

1. Do what the step asks, in the terminal, the editor or an app view.

2. Click **Check this step**, under the step's instructions. The verdict reads
   **Checking…** while Klastro inspects your environment.

3. Read the verdict:
   - **Step complete**: the step passed. Its number in the list becomes a tick
     and the progress count goes up.
   - **Not yet — try again**: at least one check failed. A short note under the
     verdict lists what did not match yet.

4. If the step failed, fix your environment and click **Check again**.

5. On the last step, click **Finish**. A **Tutorial complete. Nice work.**
   message appears.

</Steps>

:::caution[Checks are limited per step]
Each step allows a limited number of checks — three, unless the scenario's author
set a different number. Once you have used them all, further checks of that step
fail. Read the feedback and fix the environment before you check again, and do
not re-check a step that has already passed.
:::

After **Finish**:

- If you started the tutorial from a certification path, the step is marked
  complete on the path, the lab is ended, and you return to the path.
- Otherwise you return to the tutorial catalog and **the lab keeps running**.
  Pause or end it from **My Sessions** so it does not use credits needlessly. See
  [Manage your sessions](/en/learners/manage-sessions/).

## Submit a challenge

A challenge has no per-task check: it is graded once, as a whole, so its answers
cannot be found by trial and error.

<Steps>

1. Work through the tasks. Each task shows its points, and the header shows the
   scenario total.

2. On the last task, click **Finish & submit**.

3. In **Finish and submit for grading?**, click **Submit & see results**, or
   **Keep working** to go back.

4. Klastro checks every task against your environment and opens the results page,
   where each task moves from **Grading…** to **Passed** or **Not passed**.

</Steps>

Your lab keeps running after you submit, so you can go back, fix a task and
submit again. Your latest submission is the one that counts.

:::caution[Each submission uses one check per task]
Submitting checks every task once. The per-task limit (three by default) applies:
after the limit, every task in a new submission is counted as failed and your
score drops. Submit when you are confident, not to see how you are doing.
:::

See [Your results and history](/en/learners/results-history/) for the results page.

## Hints

Hints sit under a task's instructions. How they behave depends on the lab.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Tutorial">

The section is called **Guided steps** and each entry is a **Step**. Click
**Show** to read it. Guided steps are free: they never affect anything. Show them
in order, from the first.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Challenge">

The section is called **Hints** and they form a chain: only the next hint can be
opened, and the others read **Reveal hint 2 first** and so on. Click **Reveal** to
open one. The button shows what it costs, for example **(−5 pts)**.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Assessment">

Hints work as in a challenge — a chain, each with a cost — but the cost is not
shown to you. Revealing a hint still reduces your score for that task.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

Revealed hints are recorded: they stay open after a reload or on another device,
and a revealed hint cannot be hidden again.

## Scoring

Scoring applies to challenges and assessments; a tutorial is not scored.

- Each task is worth the points shown next to it. The scenario's author decides
  how they are split.
- A task's points are earned check by check: each check that passes adds its
  share, so a task that does not fully pass can still earn some points.
- The cost of every hint you revealed for a task is subtracted from that task's
  points. A task never goes below zero, so hints on one task cannot cost you
  points earned on another.
- Your score is the sum over all tasks, using the latest check of each task.

In an assessment, the recruiter sees your score and per-task results in your
report. You do not see points during the assessment.
