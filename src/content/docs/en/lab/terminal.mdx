The terminal is where you do the work in a Klastro lab. It runs in your browser
and connects to a real environment, so there is nothing to install. This page
shows how to use it day to day.

**Before you start:** open a lab and wait until the terminal stops showing
**Your environment is being prepared**. See [The lab workspace](/en/lab/overview/)
for the layout.

## Shell tabs and Node tabs

The terminal pane can hold two kinds of terminal tab.

| Tab | When it appears | Where it puts you |
|---|---|---|
| **Shell 1**, **Shell 2**… | Always | The session shell. `kubectl`, `helm` and this lab's credentials are set up here. |
| **Node**, or **Node 1**, **Node 2**… | Only when your lab has machines | A terminal directly on that machine. One tab per machine, up to five. |

In a lab with machines, the terminal opens on **Node 1**, and hovering a Shell
tab reminds you that the session shell is not one of the machines.

### Open and close shells

<Steps>

1. Click the **+** button (**New shell**) at the end of the tab strip. A new
   **Shell** tab opens and becomes active.

2. Switch between tabs by clicking them. Every tab keeps running while hidden,
   so a long command in **Shell 1** carries on while you work in **Shell 2**.

3. To close a shell, hover its tab and click the **×** that appears.

</Steps>

You can have up to five Shell tabs open at once. The **+** button disappears when
you reach the limit. The last remaining Shell tab cannot be closed, and Node tabs
cannot be closed.

### Reach a machine from the session shell

When your lab has machines, the Instructions pane has a **Connect to the
machines** section (collapsed by default). It lists one copy button per machine,
for example `ssh node1`, `ssh node2`.

<Steps>

1. Expand **Connect to the machines** in the Instructions pane.

2. Click the command for the machine you want. It is copied to your clipboard.

3. Paste it into a **Shell** tab and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

</Steps>

To run a single command on a machine without opening a session there, use the
same form with the command in quotes, as shown in that section:
`ssh node1 'kubectl get nodes'`.

:::tip
You rarely need `ssh` at all: each machine already has its own **Node** tab.
:::

## Copy and paste

The terminal uses your browser's normal copy and paste. Klastro does not ask for
permission to read your clipboard.

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="macOS">

| To | Do this |
|---|---|
| Copy text from the terminal | Select it with the mouse, then press <kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> |
| Paste into the terminal | <kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> |
| Interrupt the running command | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> |

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows / Linux">

| To | Do this |
|---|---|
| Copy text from the terminal | Select it with the mouse, then right-click and choose **Copy**, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Insert</kbd> |
| Paste into the terminal | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>, <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Insert</kbd>, or right-click and choose **Paste** |
| Interrupt the running command | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> |

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::caution[Ctrl+C is not copy on Windows and Linux]
Inside the terminal, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> interrupts the running
command, as in any Linux terminal, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> is sent to
the shell rather than pasting. Use the shortcuts in the table above.
:::

:::tip
Commands in the Instructions pane are copy buttons. Click one, then paste it in
the terminal: it is quicker and avoids typos.
:::

## Scroll back through output

If your shell shows a green status line at the bottom, it runs inside `tmux`,
which is what keeps it alive across reloads. In that case, scroll back through
earlier output with tmux's own scroll mode:

<Steps>

1. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd>, release, then press <kbd>[</kbd>.

2. Move with the arrow keys or <kbd>Page Up</kbd> / <kbd>Page Down</kbd>.

3. Press <kbd>q</kbd> to return to the prompt.

</Steps>

## Disconnects and reconnecting

### What keeps running

Your shells run in the lab, not in your browser. Closing the tab, reloading the
page or losing your network does **not** stop them: commands keep running, and
when the terminal reconnects you find the same shell with its output. Klastro
also remembers which tabs you had open, in that browser tab, and reopens them
after a reload.

:::note
Reconnecting brings back your shell and its running commands. Use the scroll
mode above, rather than the mouse wheel, to read output that was printed while
you were away.
:::

### Reconnect a terminal

The terminal does not reconnect on its own after its connection drops. When it
does, a bar appears at the bottom of the terminal reading
**Terminal disconnected** or **Terminal connection error**, and the tab's dot
turns grey or red.

<Steps>

1. Click **Reconnect** in that bar.

2. If the terminal still does not come back, reload the page. Your shells are
   still running and reattach as the tabs reopen.

</Steps>

Two other signs tell you the lab itself is waiting rather than the terminal:

- A Wi-Fi-off icon at the right of the tab strip, and **Waiting to start
  session...** in the status bar: the page has lost its live link to your lab.
  It reconnects automatically.
- **Reconnecting…** in the middle of the terminal pane, before any shell has
  appeared: the same thing, seen before the terminal first connects.

If a terminal will not reconnect at all, see
[Terminal troubleshooting](/en/troubleshooting/terminal/).

## What happens to running processes

| Situation | Running commands |
|---|---|
| You switch to another tab in the lab | Keep running |
| You reload the page or your connection drops | Keep running; the terminal reattaches to them |
| You close a Shell tab with **×** | Klastro stops showing it. Do not rely on finding it again: finish or stop long-running commands before closing their tab. |
| You pause the lab (**Pause & resume later**) | Your files and environment are kept. Check, after resuming, that any foreground command you need is still running, and start it again if not. |
| You end the lab, or it ends | Everything stops and the environment is destroyed |
